# Benchmark products

Four virtual data room products used as the reference set for this project.
Read this before running a scan.

Verify URLs on first use each session — vendor sites restructure, and a stale
path wastes a scan. If a link 404s, search for the product name plus the
capability rather than guessing at a new path.

---

## Ellty

- Site: https://www.ellty.com
- VDR product page: https://www.ellty.com/virtual-data-room
- Blog (use-case heavy, useful for scans): https://www.ellty.com/blog

**Positioning.** Document sharing and analytics platform that includes data
room functionality. Aimed at founders, small teams, and advisors rather than
enterprise deal desks. Flat monthly pricing with users included, roughly
$69–350/month depending on plan, published openly.

**Stated strengths.** Page-by-page document analytics showing time spent per
page; granular permissions; NDA gating; dynamic watermarking; real-time
engagement tracking; secure links with password protection and expiry dates.
Positions itself as fast to set up without training.

**Read this product differently.** It is a tier below the other three on price
and buyer. Where Ellty lacks an enterprise feature, that is usually a
segment decision, not a capability gap. Where Ellty has something the
enterprise three lack, it is usually a self-serve or speed-of-setup
affordance.

## iDeals

- Site: https://www.idealsvdr.com
- Support: https://www.idealsvdr.com/support/

**Positioning.** Enterprise VDR, widely used for M&A. Pricing via quote.
Emphasises customer service and ease of use alongside bank-grade security.

**Known differentiators.** Fence view (screen shielding); remote shredding;
built-in redaction that removes the need for third-party PDF tools; granular
user permission levels (sources cite between 4 and 8 levels — verify current
number rather than repeating either); advanced Q&A workflows; multilingual
interface reported at up to 15 languages; desktop app synchronisation;
detailed audit trails.

---

## Ansarada

- Site: https://www.ansarada.com
- Data room product page: https://www.ansarada.com/data-room

**Positioning.** AI-oriented VDR for due diligence and M&A. Notably publishes
pricing openly rather than requiring a demo or quote — it markets this as a
deliberate contrast with iDeals and Intralinks.

**Known differentiators.** AiQ Bidder Engagement Score, a predictive metric
that analyses bidder behaviour to forecast deal outcomes — the most distinctive
thing in this set. Deal Workflow, a dedicated project management layer for
running multiple deals. Granular access controls, built-in redaction, remote
wipe, dynamic watermarking, self-destructing files, ISO 27001 certification.

---

## Datasite

- Site: https://www.datasite.com
- Product is often listed as "Datasite Diligence"

**Positioning.** Enterprise VDR, formerly Merrill Corporation, rebranded in
2020 — older sources may call it "Merrill data room". Sell-side focused,
operating across 170+ countries. Priced at the top of this set; reviews
frequently cite cost as the main complaint.

**Known differentiators.** Redaction AI, which automatically identifies and
strips personally identifiable information and logos across large document
sets — the strongest capability in this set for high-volume deals where manual
redaction is impractical. Machine-learning models trained on a large document
corpus. Request list management within the data room. Integrated Q&A with
workflows, roles, and categories. Drag-and-drop and email-to-upload ingestion.
AI-powered translation and PDF-to-XLS conversion.

---

## Reading the set as a whole

For a strategic-differentiator scan, the three to compare against are iDeals,
Ansarada, and Datasite — they compete for the same enterprise buyer and their
differences represent genuine product bets: iDeals on document-level security
controls, Ansarada on predictive deal intelligence, Datasite on AI document
processing at volume.

Ellty is the reference for what the self-serve, transparent-pricing end of the
market considers sufficient. It is the better comparison for onboarding
friction, time-to-first-share, and pricing model.
